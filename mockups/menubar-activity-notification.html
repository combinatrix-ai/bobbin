Menu bar: “working” breath and “new reply” dot

Bobbin should be usable fire-and-forget: send, close the popover, and trust the menu bar to say still working and, later, something came back. Two independent signals on the existing template glyph: the core breathes while any thread runs, and a dot sits at the lower right while any thread holds a reply you have not opened.

Nothing here carries colour. Both signals live in the alpha of the same template image, so AppKit keeps tinting the icon for light and dark menu bars and for the open/highlighted state.

2 · The six states, closer

Each chip is the glyph at 2× so the treatment is legible on this page. The rules stay the same at 1×.

Quiet

No thread running, nothing unseen. Identical to today’s icon.

Working

≥1 thread running. The core breathes 100 → 30 → 100 % over 3.0 s. Loop stays still. No count.

New reply

≥1 thread finished (done or failed) that you have not opened since. Solid dot, lower right, 1 pt knockout.

Working + new reply

Both at once. They occupy different parts of the glyph, so they simply compose.

Popover open

State keeps updating while open. A dot that clears because you opened its thread fades out right here, in the highlighted icon.

Reduce Motion

Same states, no motion: the core rests at 50 % while working. The dot appears and disappears without the scale-in.

3 · Anatomy at 12×

All numbers are points in the 18 pt image; the mark geometry is the existing MarkSpec.standard, untouched.

Image / mark
18 pt box, 15 pt mark centred (inset 1.5) — unchanged.
Core breath
core alpha 1.0 → 0.30 → 1.0, 3.0 s per cycle (≈ 20/min, a resting breath), ease-in-out. Starts at 1.0 so the first frame equals the quiet icon; on the last thread stopping it eases back to 1.0 in ≤ 400 ms, never snaps mid-breath.
Breath, Reduce Motion
core alpha fixed at 0.5 while working. Transitions between 1.0 and 0.5 are instant.
Dot
⌀ 5 pt, centre (14.5, 14.5), solid black in template space. The loop is knocked out by a ⌀ 7 pt clear circle around it (1 pt ring), the way Finder and Dropbox badge a glyph.
Dot motion
in: 180 ms ease-out, scale 0.6 → 1 with fade. Out: 120 ms fade. Reduce Motion: fade only, 120 ms both ways.
Frames
alpha changes only, so 12 fps is enough: 36 pre-rendered template frames per cycle, swapped by a timer while working. No per-frame CPU drawing.

4 · Why the core breathes instead of the mark spinning

Rotation was the first idea and is worth rejecting explicitly. Watch both in the corner of your eye for ten seconds.

chosen

Core breath

  • Reads as “attending”, not “loading”. The loop, Bobbin’s identity, stays put; only the centre pulses.
  • Peripheral-vision friendly. Motion is confined to a 4.5 pt square at 20 cycles a minute; it registers when you look, not when you don’t.
  • Degrades honestly. Reduce Motion gets a static half-tone core: same state, still legible, no second design.
  • Same family as the in-app cue. The conversation already pulses three dots while waiting; the menu bar does the slower, quieter version.
rejected

Continuous rotation

  • It becomes a spinner. The mark is 180°-symmetric, so a rotating loop reads as a two-blade activity indicator, i.e. “stuck loading”, for possibly an hour.
  • It shimmers at 16 px. Two thick arcs with round caps alias as they cross pixel rows; the gaps flicker rather than glide.
  • The menu bar is not a place for indefinite motion. macOS’s own items sit still; a rotating one next to the clock is exactly what people install utilities to hide.
  • Reduce Motion needs a second design anyway. Rotation is the very thing it must suppress, so a static variant is required regardless; the breath already is that variant with a pulse added.
  • Costs more. Rotation needs smooth 30–60 fps redraws of the template; the breath is fine at 12 fps of alpha changes.

5 · Where the dot goes when you open Bobbin

The dot on the icon is the dot on a row. Opening that row clears both. The thread list is the inbox; the menu bar only says whether the inbox has something new.

Running keeps today’s accent dot and “Running” label: accent still means working, or kept.

New uses an ink dot in the same slot plus a semibold title. Monochrome on purpose: it is the same mark as the menu-bar dot, carried inside. Once opened the row returns to its plain form.

Failed gets the same new-dot; the metadata says “Failed”. One dot means “look”, the row says why. No red in the menu bar.

Stopped by you never gets a dot: you were there.

Multiple running threads: one breath. Multiple unseen: one dot. The menu bar answers “busy?” and “anything new?”; the list answers “which”.

7 · Accessibility

The status item stays a single button labelled “Bobbin”; state goes in its value, updated only on transitions (never per frame).

StateVoiceOver value
quiet— (no value)
working“1 thread working” / “3 threads working”
new reply“1 new reply” / “2 new replies”
both“1 thread working, 1 new reply”

Reduce Motion follows NSWorkspace.accessibilityDisplayShouldReduceMotion and its change notification, so flipping the setting mid-run switches treatments without restart. Increase Contrast and Differentiate Without Color need nothing extra: the treatment is monochrome alpha.

8 · What needs a decision

A. When does the dot clear? Proposed: per thread, when you open that thread (mail-style “unread”). Alternative: on any popover open (“seen the list”). The alternative is simpler but loses the marker for threads you did not read, which defeats fire-and-forget with several threads.

B. Failures share the dot. Proposed: yes, one monochrome dot; the row says “Failed”. Alternative: a hollow ring for failure, which is hard to read at ⌀ 5 pt.
